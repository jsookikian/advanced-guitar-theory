How to use Half Diminished / Minor 6 arpeggios to play over dominant chords

The 9 approach

Five arpeggio shapes that put a half diminished or minor 6 chord on top of a C dominant. The arpeggio tones are E, G, Bb and D. Each diagram also marks C, the root of the dominant chord, as a reference.

How it works

Over C9, play Eø (E G Bb D) from the 3rd, or Gm6 (G Bb D E) from the 5th. Both give you the 3, 5, b7 and 9 of the chord.

In the original arpeggio diagrams, open rings are C, the root of the dominant chord. Filled dots are the other arpeggio notes. Note names are added; the original had plain dots. Strings run high e at the top to low E at the bottom, frets are numbered under each diagram.

The five shapes

Each shape is shown with its full diagram first. The full diagram is yours as written. The highlight diagram marks the Drop 2 voicing on the upper four strings (D, G, B, e) that the label refers to.

Under each Drop 2, C Mixolydian adds the full seven-note scale: C D E F G A Bb. Each scale diagram shows every scale note in the same fret window, not a prescribed fingering. Teal marks the same four Drop 2 notes, open rings mark C, and the other filled dots complete the scale.
